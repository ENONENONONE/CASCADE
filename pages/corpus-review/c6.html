The other side of the serial

The mirror is a two-way thing. Outbound, the ⇄ MIRROR button writes the browser's field state to the device every 200 ms: the sixteen most energetic positions as $F, the same as glyphs as $G, each one's doubling and halving as $D, its tunnel chain as $T, the single ×55 tunnel multiplier as $M, and the twelve ray energies as $R. Inbound, the device answers JSON commands, the ↓ DUMP button sends the full set, and the BLE path reads the device's own mirror from the Cascade GATT characteristic ending 0003 as a list of positions with energies. On the evening of 2026-09-04 the inbound side was read directly, with the machine page closed so that the port was free.

The node was an ESP32-C6, Espressif USB JTAG/serial debug unit, vendor 0x303A product 0x1001, serial number — at 115200 baud, the rate the machine opens. Chrome held the port until it was closed. The port was then opened with DTR and RTS both asserted, the one state the USB-Serial-JTAG peripheral does not read as a reset, and closed by dropping RTS before DTR; the node did not restart. Its first unsolicited bytes were the tail of a $M frame at tick 44732, so MIRROR had been live from the machine until the moment Chrome closed. The firmware calls itself bwave: it logs CSI frames on channel 4, forwards to a display over uart_cyd, stores tunnel positions per device seen, and runs an accelerometer fall detector, which fired twice during the read.

The field seen from the far end

The cascade replies compute a position on the device. Every dlog, ray, residue class, negation, chart fold and inverse agreed with the tablet: 83 with dlog 15, fold 173, neg 174, inv 96; 5 with dlog 55, fold 251, neg 252, inv 103; 252 with dlog 183, fold 4, neg 5, inv 154. The device therefore uses both folds, and names them: fold is 256 − n and neg is 257 − n. Its five extra fields are the framework's own quantities, and three of them were identified exactly from the three samples, a fourth as a ratio, and one not at all:

at a = 5, 83, 252 trace = 2a 10, 166, 247 impedance = a · 377 ≡ a · 120 86, 194, 171 Z = a · Z₀, with Z₀ = 377 Ω = F(14) ≡ 120 GOLDEN RATIO reflection = (a − 377) · (a + 377)⁻¹ 143, 34, 133 Γ = (Z − Z₀)/(Z + Z₀), the genome's reflection coefficient, the position as Z quality = a · (256 − a)⁻¹ 42, 204, 63 a position over its chart-fold partner norm = ? 174, 113, 234 fits −2a² − 6a − 3 on the three samples; any three points fit a quadratic

The device's pi field gave 9, 2 and 5 at positions 5, 83 and 252. Those are the digits of π at those indices counted from the leading 3, so the firmware carries the correct π where the machine's embedded p column, item 20 of the register, reads 6 at 83. Its word table is a third edition, distinct from both the JSON tablet and the machine's dictionary: 83 is BISMUTH, which is the element whose atomic number is 83 and the word added to the tablet at 83 earlier the same day; 5 is HAMSA, 173 HAFNIUM, 154 YTTERBIUM, 251 HOLY OF HOLIES, 103 PRAYING MANTIS; 4 is BAA and 252 EINSTEIN FIELD EQUATIONS as in the tablet. Also on the wire, not asked for: a CSI table of 32 devices seen, the two busiest at about 35,000 frames each, and a tunnel store that had reached 3,331 devices. Their addresses are not reproduced here.

The FLIP control

The mirror as written was one-way: the $F stream is write-only and nothing in the machine parsed a reply on it, so the other side could only be seen by going around, as the readout above did from a shell. On 2026-09-04 the machine was given the same move as a control. A button labelled ◐ FLIP now sits in the CSI serial row at line 909, between ⇄ MIRROR and ×2, and calls serialBus.flipMirror() at line 15275. It uses only two commands the firmware already answers, so nothing on the device changes and nothing needs to be flashed.

  • With no port open it reports "FLIP: no serial device — connect first" in the status field and stops.
  • If MIRROR is live it pauses the 200 ms outbound frames for the duration of the read, so the reply is not interleaved with the device's echoes, and restarts them in a finally block whatever happens.
  • It sends {"cmd":"flip_status"} and prints the flip state, the x-ray value and the three flip counters; then the NanO₂ block with two checks made in the browser, pub × inv ≡ 1 and fold = 257 − pub, each marked ✓ or ✗; then the device's top positions with word and energy, the device's dlog and ray checked against the machine's own dlog table, a mismatch printing our value beside theirs.
  • It sends {"cmd":"mirror_broadcast"} and prints how many positions the device holds and broadcasts.
  • Everything renders in the main terminal trace in the machine's gold, glow and amber classes, and the parsed result is kept as serialBus._otherSide for other panels. This edition of v4 has no log-out pane, so DUMP in this copy writes only to the console; FLIP does not depend on it.

Against the C6 read that evening the button would print 136, 240, 121 and 72 with every check passing and "48 positions held and broadcast by the device". The whole inline script parses under node after the edit; the button was not pressed against the live device from this side, so its first real press is RITO's. The edit added 53 lines and the pre-edit file is kept as a backup.
