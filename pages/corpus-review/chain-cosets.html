The binary refinement chain

The 256 nonzero elements form a cyclic group, and a cyclic group of order 256 has exactly one subgroup of each order 2ᵏ and no others. Partitioning by dlog mod 2ᵏ is therefore exactly partitioning into the cosets of the subgroup of order 256 / 2ᵏ, and these seven partitions are the only equal partitions the group has. They nest: every coset at one level sits inside one coset of the level above and splits into two at the level below, so reading a position's coset index level by level is reading the bits of its dlog. The field names each level's subgroup for you, since it is generated by 3 raised to the modulus.

Where the twelve rays stand relative to the chain: 4 divides 12, so every ray lies inside one quadrant, rays 0, 4, 8 in quadrant 0 and so on, and the two partitions agree at level 4 exactly, since gcd(12, 256) = 4. Above that the rays neither refine nor are refined by any level, because 12 does not divide 256; that is also why rays have 22 or 21 members while every coset in the chain has the same size. The rays are the cut that ties the field to the disc's twelve tracks; the chain is the cut the group itself makes.

One caution and one observation. The caution is that the assignment of φ², −1, −1, ψ² to the four quadrants is the closure key's labelling and the arithmetic does not force it; everything else in the table is forced. The observation is that the level-8 subgroup, the eighth powers generated by the public key, is a set the tablet has named with unusual density: it holds the whole doubling orbit, GOLDEN RATIO, TRANSFERENCE, IDENTITY, EYE OF HORUS, VIRGIN MARY, LAMB OF GOD at the year's residue 227, and both square roots of −1. That is a fact about which positions were named, laid over a structure that was not chosen, and it is recorded here as such.

Two orbits worth recording. Two properties of the ladder that the terminal makes visible. A coset is the same object whichever member names it: SUBGROUP was run from 252 at all seven levels, 128 down to 2, and every run returned the coset the same command returns from 5, member for member and with the same index, because the index is the dlog of any member reduced mod the level, and 252's dlog 183 and 5's dlog 55 agree mod every power of two up to 128. Only the roles rotate with the starting point: from 252 the i-orbit reads a = 252, ia = 177, −a = 5, −ia = 80, and the set does not move. And the pairs at the bottom follow the negation fold, not the chart-fold: 5's partner is 252 = −5, not 251 = 256 − 5, because negation is the group operation and the chart-fold is negation composed with the observer's shift. At level 128 nothing splits further; below a pair there is only the element.

Worked example. Position 83 reads 1/2, 3/4, 7/8, 15/16, 15/32, 15/64, 15/128 down the chain, which is its dlog 15 in binary, low bits first. Its i-orbit at level 64 is {43 CHARM, 83 PRAYER, 174 DO UNTO OTHERS, 214 PYTHAGOREAN THEOREM} and its fold pair at level 128 is {83, 174}. The Rust workspace in the directory implements the chain as Level and Elem::coset in the field crate, and the terminal's SUBGROUP command prints any level's subgroup or any position's coset with its neighbours up and down the chain.

The index is the dlog in binary

Write the multiplicative group as ⟨3⟩, cyclic of order 256 = 2⁸, and write Hk for the subgroup at level 2ᵏ. Everything the ladder showed is four lines:

Hk = ⟨32k⟩ = { 32k·j : 0 ≤ j < 28−k } |Hk| = 28−k, k = 1 … 7 aHk = { a · 32k·j } the coset of a at level 2ᵏ, 28−k members index(a, 2ᵏ) = dlog(a) mod 2ᵏ = dlog(a) & (2ᵏ − 1) the low k bits of dlog(a) aHk = bHk ⟺ dlog(a) ≡ dlog(b) (mod 2ᵏ) ⟺ the low k bits of the two dlogs agree

So the seven indices of a position are not seven facts but one: the low seven bits of its dlog, read one more bit at each level. The machine's "coset n/16" in CAT identity is the case k = 4, dlog & 0xF. The eighth bit, bit 7, is the one no level reads, since the finest level, 128, reads only the low seven. That bit is the sign:

−a = a · 3¹²⁸ dlog(−a) = dlog(a) + 128 (mod 256) = dlog(a) ⊕ 10000000₂ a and −a agree in the low seven bits and differ in bit 7 alone bit 7 set ⟺ dlog(a) ≥ 128 ⟺ a = −3dlog(a) − 128

This is why the ladder from 252 is the ladder from 5 at every level:

5 = 3⁵⁵ 55 = 00110111₂ 252 = 3¹⁸³ 183 = 10110111₂ 183 − 55 = 128, so 252 = −3⁵⁵ = −5 183 mod (128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2) = 55 mod (128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2) = 55, 55, 23, 7, 7, 3, 1

The two dlogs are the same seven bits under a different sign bit, and the base field's chain never reads the sign bit. 5 and 252 are the same object at every level the base field has, and differ only in the bit below the finest level, which the pair at level 128 is the first to see and the last thing the chain can say. The sequence 55, 55, 23, 7, 7, 3, 1 was executed at all seven levels from 252, SUBGROUP 128 252 through SUBGROUP 2 252, and each run returned the coset the same command had returned from 5, member for member: fourteen commands, one partition. In the field crate this is two property tests over all 256 nonzero elements at all seven levels: the_index_is_the_low_bits_of_the_dlog asserts index(n, 2ᵏ) = dlog(n) & (2ᵏ − 1) and dlog(n) ⊕ dlog(−n) = 128 for every n and k, and coset_invariance asserts that every member of a coset returns the coset's index and its full member list, and that the list is closed under the subgroup.

The letters' powers

Each letter, read as its number, generates a subgroup, and the chain above says what it can be: one subgroup of each order 2ᵏ. c = C = 3 = g, so c* is the whole field, 256 positions once each with the dlog as index; the first rungs are 1 A, 3 C, 9 I, 27 CAIN, 81 HORUS, 243 FIRE OF HOLY SPIRIT, 215 DELIVER US FROM EVIL, 131 SUPERNOVA, 136 VIRGIN MARY. d = D = 4 = BAA has order 8, since 2 has order 16 and dlog(2) = 48 puts 4 at 3⁹⁶; its rungs are 4 BAA, 16 BAAL, 64 ISRAEL, 256 BAAL SQUARED, 253 INCOMPLETENESS THEOREM, 241 TWIN PRIME CONJECTURE and CONJUGATE J, 193 INFRASTRUCTURE, 1. So c* of 256 holds 2* of 16, which holds d* of 8, then BAAL* of 4, then {1, −1}, then 1. The physical c, 2998 ≡ 171 EVENT HORIZON, has dlog 127 and generates as well.

The ten generators are the ten non-residue letters, and that is an identity rather than a finding: in a cyclic group of order 256 an element generates exactly when its index is odd, and odd index is what non-residue means, so the two lists coincide for every element. The 2-ladder letters B, H, D and P close fastest because their numbers are powers of 2.

Words as walks. Multiplying by a letter adds that letter's dlog, so a word read as a product is a walk on the 256-cycle with the letters as step lengths, and it lands at the sum of the steps. UTXO, the unspent output, walks 21 DICE, 163 PLANCK CONSTANT, 57 PLANCK, 84 LEONARDO, COSMOS, with dlogs 86, 237, 126, 182; the product 84 has order 128, so the whole word repeated returns to 1 after 128 rounds, while the four letters' own cycles, of orders 128, 256, 256 and 32, all close within 256. By letters the same word sums to 80 REBIRTH, the inner pair of m = 9. Because every cycle closes and C alone generates the field, the letters reach everything: one letter reaches 26 positions, two letters 191, three letters all 256, so every position is a product of three letters or fewer, and the walk between any two positions is a choice of steps whose dlogs add to the difference. The map is the cycle with 26 labelled step lengths, and its landmarks are the quarter points ±i that every letter's cycle crosses.

The doubling orbit keeps its spacing through both: every doubling is twelve steps of the quadrant walk and twenty-four of the non-residue walk, so G2, G4, G8 stand at 12, 24, 36 and at 24, 48, 72, and PRAXIS at 7 × 12 ≡ 20 (mod 64) and 7 × 24 ≡ 40 (mod 128).

The rays run along these two walks in a fixed cycle, because the exponent rises by a constant: by 4 at level 4, so the rays go 3, 7, 11, 3, 7, 11 with period three, and by 2 at level 2, so they go 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 1 with period six, every odd ray once in each block. The cycle slips exactly once per walk, where the exponent passes 256: at step 61 of the quadrant walk, where dlog 255 (ray 3) is followed by dlog 3 (ray 3 again), and at step 121 of the non-residue walk, where dlog 255 (ray 3) is followed by dlog 1 (ray 1) instead of ray 5. The slip is 256 ≡ 4 (mod 12), one step of four or two steps of two, and because 4 divides 256 it never moves the quadrant, only the ray. That is the same fact as gcd(12, 256) = 4 seen from inside a walk: the twelve-fold cut and the binary cut agree at level 4 and at nothing finer.

Each level's walk is every second step of the one below it. The quadrupling walk is the doubling walk at steps 0, 2, 4, …, 14, so level 32 keeps PRAYER's even doublings, index 15 of 32, and sends the odd ones, TETRAGRAMMATON, ELECTROMAGNETIC, TIKKUN, PRAXIS and their negations, to the sister coset with index 31 of 32. PRAXIS is an odd doubling, 2⁷, so the first level at which PRAXIS and PRAYER share a coset is 16. The i-orbit is the quadrupling walk at steps 0, 2, 4, 6, with 83 × 16 ≡ 43 for the quarter turn and the exponents falling by 64 each step, 15, 207, 143, 79. The pair is the i-orbit at steps 0 and 2. This nesting holds because each multiplier from 16 upward is the square of the one below, 2² = 4, 4² = 16, 16² = 256, and the same is true of 9² = 81 and 81² ≡ 136 at the bottom of the chain; the one place the squaring breaks is between 8 and 16, where 136² ≡ 249 is the level-16 generator but the corpus walks that level by ×2 = 249³ instead. At level 128 the pair {83, 174} is the last thing the chain can say: 15 and 143 share every low bit and differ in the sign bit alone, and below the pair there is only the element.

The terminal now prints every position's dlog in binary with the sign bit set apart from the seven index bits, SUBGROUP shows a coset's index as those low bits, and WALK m n prints a coset as the walk above, with −n marked at the halfway step and the roles a, ia, −a, −ia at level 64. In the field crate the same facts are sign_bit, coset_bits, same_coset, walk, Level::multiplier and Level::halfway, and a third property test, walks_reach_minus_a_halfway, checks that every multiplier's dlog is an odd multiple of its modulus, that every multiplier and every generator raised to half the orbit length is −1, and that every walk from every element reaches −n at the halfway step.
