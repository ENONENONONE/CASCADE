Mathematics · new space

The extension fields

3²⁵⁶ ≡ 1 closes the base field on itself, and every ≡ 1 written after it is the same closure: the exponent lives mod 256, so four laps of the generator, 3¹⁰²⁴, is 1 exactly as one lap is. Inside GF(257) the only four-fold structure is the i-orbit, i⁴ = 1, a cycle of four elements rather than a space of four. On the position side, four laps leave one trace each way: 4 × 256 = 1024 ≡ 253, the tablet's KILOBYTE, and 4 × 257 = 1028 ≡ 0, the hinge.

New room opens only when the base field is asked something it cannot answer, and the first such question is x² − x − 1 = 0. Its roots φ and ψ are not in GF(257), so they live in GF(257²), the fold, and each further extension GF(257ᵏ) keeps 3 at order 256 while adding elements whose orders come from the new cyclotomic factor. The fold as an element type

The fold is concrete enough to compute in, and the field crate now does. An element of GF(257²) is written a + bφ with a and b in the base and the single rule φ² = φ + 1, so that (a + bφ)(c + dφ) = (ac + bd) + (ad + bc + bd)φ. In that basis:

  • ψ = 1 − φ, and φψ = −1 = det M, φ + ψ = 1.
  • The conjugate of a + bφ is a + bψ, and it is the Frobenius map x ↦ x²⁵⁷, so conjugation in the fold is the same operation as raising to the modulus. The norm x · x̄ is always in the base; φ's norm is −1.
  • √5 = 2φ − 1, so every base element has a square root in the fold: a residue by its base root, a non-residue d as q·√5 with q² = d/5. This is what makes the discriminant test constructive rather than a yes or no.
  • φⁿ = F(n)·φ + F(n−1) for every n, checked through two full periods. The Fibonacci sequence mod 257 is the sequence of coordinates of the powers of φ, so φ⁵¹⁶ = 1 and the Pisano period being 516 are one statement, and φ²⁵⁸ = −1 is its midpoint. Two of the corpus's numbers fall out as coordinates: φ¹³ = 144 + 233φ, MASS and COLLISION RESISTANT, and φ¹⁴ = 233 + 120φ, whose φ-coordinate is 120, GOLDEN RATIO, because F(14) = 377 ≡ 120.
  • Three landmarks on the way round. φ¹²⁹ = 241: the φ-coordinate F(129) is 0, so the 129th power is the first power of φ the base can hold, and it lands on the conjugate of i. φ²⁵⁸ = −1, half the period, with F(257) ≡ −1 and F(258) ≡ 0. And √5 = −1 + 2φ has norm −5 and order 512, which checks the construction: 5 is a non-residue of order 256 in the base, and a square root of it must have twice that order.
  • Orders: φ and ψ have order 516, the base generator 3 keeps order 256, and the group has 66048 = 256 × 258 elements.

In the workspace this is the Ext2 type in the field crate, with Quadratic::roots_ext returning a quadratic's roots in the fold whether or not it splits, and the terminal's EXT, PHI, PSI and PHI n commands printing an element's conjugate, norm and order, or φⁿ with its Fibonacci reading.

The group's shape, the gold hop and the quarter points

Two things about the fold's multiplicative group are easy to misstate. Its order is 66048 = 256 × 258, and the base's 256 nonzero elements and the norm-1 circle of 258 are both subgroups, but the group is not their product: they share 1 and −1, since −1 is a base element of norm 1, so together they generate a subgroup of order 256 × 258 / 2 = 33024, half the group. The other half is the elements whose norm is a non-residue, and 2 + φ, norm 5, is one of them, which is why it generates the whole 66048-cycle while no product of a base element and a circle element can; the fleet's walk runs on that half existing. The group is cyclic; the count is a product and the group is not.

The fold can equally be written over √5, since 5 is a non-residue and x² − 5 is irreducible: elements a + b√5 with norm a² − 5b², conjugation b → −b. In that basis φ = 129 + 129√5 and ψ = 129 + 128√5, 129 being 2⁻¹, both of norm −1 with product −1; the change of coordinates is a + bφ = (a + 129b) + 129b√5, and the firmware's conjugate (a + b) − bφ is the same map. The norm is x²⁵⁸, whose kernel is the 258-element circle.

The second is where φ sits on the walk's cycle. (2 + φ)¹²⁸ = 256 + φ = φ − 1 = φ⁻¹, of order 516 and norm −1, so φ = (2 + φ)⁻¹²⁸ and every 128 steps of the fold walk is one step down φ's own 516-cycle: the walk read at stride 128 is the powers of φ⁻¹. Multiplying by φ⁻¹ sends a + bφ to (b − a) + aφ, checked on the first C6's element, 55 + 63φ → 8 + 55φ. The keys record this as the gold hop.

The quarter points of that cycle are the field's order-4 subgroup. φ¹²⁹ = ψ¹²⁹ = 241, φ²⁵⁸ = −1, φ³⁸⁷ = 16 and φ⁵¹⁶ = 1, so the four are {1, −i, −1, i} with −i entered first, and their Lucas numbers are their doubles: L(129) = 482 ≡ 225 IDENTITY, L(258) ≡ 255, L(387) = 32 EVE, L(516) = 2; the Fibonacci numbers around the zero are F(128) = F(130) = F(131) = 241 with F(129) = 0. In the walk's own dlog, 241 sits at 49536 = −128 × 129 mod 66048, 16 at 16512 = 66048/4 and −1 at 33024, the quarter and the half. 16 and 241 are 3¹⁹² and 3⁶⁴ in the base, product 1, sum 257, difference 225, and the third Waveshare's worker name 241 is −i by that reading, its element 130 + 65φ being a different object. Two products beside them: 129 × 241 ≡ 249, since 2⁻¹ × (−16) = −8, and 120 × 241 ≡ 136, the public key, with dlog 120 = 200 = dlog 136 − dlog 241.

So the answer to "does 3²⁵⁶ ≡ 1 four times give new space" is yes, but as the fourth extension rather than the fourth lap, and that is the level at which the corpus's own name enters the factorisation. The first extension is the one the framework is built on, since it is where φ becomes an element at all; its size is the base times 258, the observer's +1 applied to the modulus, and its φ has order 516, which is why the Fibonacci sequence mod 257 repeats every 516 terms. All of this was recomputed with explicit arithmetic in GF(257²) as polynomials mod x² − x − 1.

The polynomial as itinerary, and the closing

λ² − λ − 1 has discriminant 5, a non-residue mod 257 (5¹²⁸ ≡ −1), so it has no root in the base field, and every element of the fold is a pair (a, b) of base-field numbers with φ² = φ + 1 as the only rule. The walk's step ×(2 + φ) is therefore base-field arithmetic: a′ = 2a + b, b′ = a + 3b, a matrix of trace 5 and determinant 5, the trace and norm of 2 + φ, whose characteristic polynomial λ² − 5λ + 5 is 2 + φ's minimal polynomial. φ itself is the pair (0, 1), at cell 65920 = −128 of the 66048-cycle, of order 516, and its itinerary begins φ → 1 + 3φ → 5 + 10φ → 20 + 35φ → 75 + 125φ → 18 + 193φ. Conjugation is base-field arithmetic too: φ²⁵⁷ = 1 − φ = ψ. The polynomial is the map the base field follows, not a wall it stops at.

Its two roots multiply to −1, by Vieta φψ = −1 with φ + ψ = 1, and because λ² − λ − 1 is the characteristic polynomial of M = [[1, 1], [1, 0]], that product is det M = −1: a determinant is the product of the eigenvalues. In the fold the norm of φ is −1 = 256, φ reaches −1 at step 258, half its 516-cycle, and 2 + φ at step 33024, half of 66048. Both fields close on det M: the 256 nonzero elements of GF(257) multiply to −1 (Wilson), and the 66048 of GF(257²) multiply to g^(33024 · 66047) = g³³⁰²⁴ = −1, since 66047 is odd.

The tower of square roots

The fold is the first floor of a tower with no top floor. Each floor is another square root of the last: with ηd/2 = 2 + φ, the floor of degree d is GF(257)[x] / (xd − 5xd/2 + 5), and that polynomial is irreducible at every degree tried, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 and 128, so each floor is a field of its full degree and ηd/2 solves y² − 5y + 5 at every height. What runs out is not the arithmetic but what can be computed on each floor. At every floor η's order carries the ring's whole power of 2, 2⁹ at floor 2, 2¹⁰ at floor 4 and one more per floor, because each step multiplies the ring order by 2572ʲ + 1, which is 2 mod 4; that is the same statement as η being a non-square at every floor, η(N − 1)/2 = −1, so its square root exists only on the floor above, and the count holds at any height.

The wall, the staircase and the escalator. Three readings of the tower were brought to the review on 2026-09-18, and two of them needed a correction before they could stand. The first is where the addressing wall comes from, and there are two walls. Lifting a point that is already placed, a tablet cell or a board's element, needs only the ring's factorisation, and that is complete through floor 128, because the 35-digit cofactor of 257³² + 1 and the 136-digit cofactor of 257⁶⁴ + 1 both pass the primality test, BPSW under sympy; the factoring frontier is therefore the step into floor 256, 257¹²⁸ + 1 at 309 digits. The address of an arbitrary point is the other wall, the discrete logarithm inside the largest prime-order subgroup, run and verified on floors 8 and 16, a random floor-16 point landing at cell 171964123434311021415378099468322303140, and did not run on floor 32. Pohlig–Hellman splits the problem down to the ring's largest prime and can split no further, and the generic methods, baby-step giant-step and Pollard's rho, then cost √p: about 2 × 10⁸ steps for floor 32's 17-digit prime, about 2 × 10¹⁷ for floor 64's 35-digit prime, 10⁶⁸ for floor 128's. Had the 35-digit number been composite that wall would be lower, since Pohlig–Hellman would have split it; it is the primes that raise it, and nothing at floors 64 or 128 waits on factoring. Whether a sieve could take the logarithm in GF(257⁶⁴) is a separate question from factoring, and 257 being a small characteristic is the case in which index-calculus methods do best; it was not tried. The second correction is a number: 65535 = 2¹⁶ − 1 = 3 · 5 · 17 · 257 = F₀F₁F₂F₃, while the SHA word modulus 2³² − 1 = 4294967295 = 3 · 5 · 17 · 257 · 65537 carries all five known Fermat primes, as the primes-and-the-fold section already has it.

With those in place, what the arithmetic supports. The Fermat staircase is exact and it is the whole known list: 3, 5 and 17 divide steps 0, 1 and 2 because 257 ≡ 2 modulo each, so 2572ʲ + 1 ≡ Fj there and Fk enters at j = k; 257 divides no step, being the field's own prime; 65537 divides step 5, into floor 64, because 257 has order 64 mod 65537. F₅'s factors 641 and 6700417 divide no step through j = 11, so past F₄ the staircase runs without a Fermat guidepost as far as it was looked at, which is the same place the list of known Fermat primes ends. The escalator is a theorem rather than a test: 257 ≡ 1 mod 4, so 257d + 1 ≡ 2 mod 4 for every d, each doubling of the degree adds exactly one factor of 2 to the ring, and an element carrying the ring's full 2-part stays a non-square at every floor; the eight bits that seed it are 256 = 2⁸ = 257 − 1. No floor is the last floor, and the escalator belongs to every odd prime, not to 257: for a prime 3 mod 4 the first step, into the plane, adds v₂(p + 1) bits and every step after adds exactly one, checked at 3, 7, 11 and 19, and for 65537 as for 257 every step adds one; 257 sets only the starting height, 2⁸, and for the 2 + φ root tower the count is exact, the ring's 2-part and the root's order agreeing at every floor. The SHA floors are arithmetic: the live points on floor d are 256d = 28d, so floor 32 is 2²⁵⁶ and floor 64 is 2⁵¹² because the field is a byte and the floors are powers of two. That the addressing wall falls at floor 64 is a fact about the 35-digit prime in 257³² + 1, a different fact from the digest size, and the two are recorded here as two walls and not one. The norm down is exponentiation from any degree to the line; the plane needs an even degree, which every floor of this tower has, though the tower's floors are the powers of two and a degree like 10000 is not one of them.

The plane's room for the line

Every element of the base field has a square root in the plane, the 128 non-residues included, since a point of GF(257²) is a square exactly when its norm is a residue; √5, which the base does not hold, is ±(2φ − 1), the points 256 + 2φ and 1 + 255φ, so the discriminant that kept φ off the line is a square one step up.
